The Orpo government has failed

Orpo’s National Coalition Party came to power promising to halt the growth of public debt and to create 100,000 jobs. At the same time, it fiercely attacked the previous government over exactly these issues.

So I thought I would look at how the two governments have performed in the light of the statistics. For clarity, the charts have been made consistent so that a value going down means things are getting worse. Let’s start with the promised jobs, beginning with employment.

The opposition and the media grilled Marin’s government relentlessly over a lack of “employment measures”, even though during Marin’s term employment rose to its highest level in over 30 years. By the end of the previous government’s term there were about 110,000 more people in work than at its start.

The Orpo government, on the other hand, has beyond any doubt failed to deliver on its promise of 100,000 new jobs: there are now about 47,000 fewer people in work than when it took office. Instead, Finland has risen to the top of the EU in unemployment. So let’s look at unemployment.

During Orpo’s term, the unemployment rate has risen from 7.2 to 10.5 per cent. And what about halting the growth of debt?

During Orpo’s term, the debt ratio has risen from 74.8 to 90.3 per cent. That is about twice as much as over the whole previous term, even though it was hit by the pandemic.

Beyond the promises, it is worth looking at everyday life.

Almost 50 per cent more bankruptcies are being filed than in the previous term: about 312 a month instead of about 212.

In 2024, about 137,000 children lived in low-income families, about 14,000 more than a year earlier. The low-income rate among children rose to 13.8 per cent, the highest in the whole series, which begins in 2010. And according to the forecasts, child poverty will grow significantly further. Yle reports (our translation):

Experts are worried about how the government’s social security cuts affect Finnish children and their development.

The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health admits in its new impact assessments that the cuts lead to more child poverty than previously thought.

According to the new assessments, the low-income rate among children will rise by three percentage points: the cuts will push 31,000 children below the poverty line.

In the previous term, homelessness fell by about a quarter. During Orpo’s term it has returned almost to its 2019 level.

Confidence in the economy collapsed in 2022 with Russia’s war of aggression, and during Orpo’s term it has come nowhere near its earlier level.

The Orpo government promised jobs and an end to the growth of debt. In the light of the statistics, it has failed on both. You can find Markkinavihreät’s alternatives among our proposals.
